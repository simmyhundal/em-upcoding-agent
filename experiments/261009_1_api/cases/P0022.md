# P0022: P0022 (Family Practice, GA): 99215s fall mostly on complex patients, but the 99215 rate is about twice peers at every complexity level; documentation is too thin to judge

**Leaning (advisory):** Inconclusive

## Why

P0022 billed 16 of 61 claims as 99215 (26.2%), against 7.2% expected for this panel's complexity (z=3.18 vs peers; risk-adjusted score 2.17). The 99215s do concentrate on sicker patients. Fourteen of 16 went to patients with 3 or more chronic conditions, and only 1 went to a patient with 0–1 conditions (C0011177: hyperlipidemia with abdominal pain). However, the provider's 99215 share is about 2–3x the all-provider rate within every complexity stratum (e.g., 3 conditions 26.7% vs 8.7%; 5+ conditions 66.7% vs 29.8%). So panel acuity does not fully explain the excess. Sampled 99215s also cluster on one patient: P0022-N0014 accounts for C0011119, C0011136, C0011139 and C0011169. Documentation evidence is limited. Only 3 billed 99215s were reviewed. One was unsupported (C0011136, documented moderate MDM, 36 min) and one was supported (C0011152, high MDM, 51 min). That 33% unsupported rate vs 24.2% for all providers rests on n=3 and cannot carry a conclusion. The 99214 documentation (1 of 11 unsupported, 9.1% vs 8.4%) looks in line with peers. Some lower-level claims on low-complexity patients may also merit a look (C0011174: 99214, no chronic conditions, R10.9; C0011164: 99214, single condition). Overall, the pattern is consistent with a complex panel plus a possible above-peer tendency to bill 99215, but the evidence does not support a firm leaning either way.
