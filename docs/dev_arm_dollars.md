# Release 4: every arm on the development seeds, in dollars (issue 108)

[Back to the README](../README.md)

Generated by `python eval/dev_arm_dollars.py`. Free. **Release 5 groundwork, not release 4.** Release 4 sticks to the points-based reinforcement learning (reward +1 per upcoder found, -1 per honest doctor flagged, -0.05 per extra round sent to an honest practice); the dollar reward and everything trained on it moves to release 5 (issue 109), where the value of a call becomes the 90% lower bound of the extrapolated estimate. **Net dollars = what the arm would demand from the upcoders it calls (extrapolated from its own random charts) minus what it costs: request rounds, burden on honest practices, chart reviews, and a charge for each honest doctor called.** The cost parameters and their sources are in `eval/dollar_reward.py`: $90 per round for copied records (CMS Transmittal 786: $0.15 a page, up to $15 a record), $43.84 per round to an honest practice (Premier 2022 average cost of fighting a denial), $158 per practice reviewed (GAO-16-394: Recovery Auditors, $158 per review), and $2,000 per honest doctor called (**an assumption**; no published figure found). L and GL are fitted on other seeds only. **Read the dollars as a ranking, not a business case:** synthetic providers have about 240 claims a year, far fewer than a real practice, so extrapolated recoveries are small beside per-round costs (see the break-even scale).

## Reading

- **Value is the extrapolated estimate, so an arm is paid only for what it can document.** A provider called on targeted charts only, or with no random charts, adds nothing, however right the call.
- **Cloned-notes upcoders are worth little in this score, and that is realistic:** their charts support the billed level, so an estimate extrapolated from their charts is about zero. Rules 2.3 find many more of them (more upcoders found), but the dollars cannot be demanded from chart review alone; they would need the audit trail or another way to document the overbilling. That is why the wider list finds more upcoders yet adds little value in dollars.
- **Break-even scale** is total costs divided by total value: values would have to be that many times larger (a bigger real practice, a longer look-back) for the arm to break even. Below 1.0x the arm pays for itself as simulated.
- **The cost figures are published proxies, not this project's costs:** per-review and per-denial figures are from other settings and years (see `eval/dollar_reward.py`). The honest-call charge is an assumption, shown at $0 and $10,000 beside the base case.
- **Same caution as the points score:** the learned variants were fitted to a reward (in points), not to these dollars, and only cross-validated rows are fair; the fresh test is the real comparison.
