## Reading

- **The frozen screen cannot see the habits.** `eb_ratio_t` (the 99215 share against expected, used by workflow v2.3 to v2.5 and agent v3.3 to v3.5) lists 18 of 88 (20%) of the upcoders in its top 10%: 0 of 33 (0%) systematic, 17 of 33 (52%) opportunistic, 1 of 22 (5%) template-driven. Opportunistic upcoders reach 99215 from 99214 at the borderline, so the 99215 share moves; the other habits do not move it.
- **A whole-distribution indicator at the 99214 boundary finds them.** `eb_upper_t` (the shrunk share billed 99214 or 99215 against what the patient mix and the test line predict) lists 81 of 88 (92%): 33 of 33 (100%) systematic, 26 of 33 (79%) opportunistic, 22 of 22 (100%) template-driven, and holds 96% of the true overpayment dollars. Without the test line (`eb_upper_c`, count only) it is 70 of 88 (80%); the gap between them is the lab link below.
- **The systematic caution holds up.** Against honest providers whose provider effect is within 0.25 of the upcoder's own, the best indicators outscore nearly all of them (table above), so the systematic gain is not only the planted panel shift. The 99215-share screens, by contrast, rank systematic upcoders no better than chance (AUC about 0.5).
- **By construction.** The generator draws a visit's lab line from the TRUE complexity (a lab is 90% likely when the true data element is high, 10% otherwise). `hi_no_test` (high level with nothing after it) therefore finds 88 of 88 (100%): a planted covariate, not evidence that real claims behave so. Every indicator that uses the test line (`eb_upper_t`, `pay_resid`, `drift`, `rich_level_t`, the rankers) leans on it; `eb_upper_c`, `level_z` and `same_patient` do not (`same_patient` also needs shared patients, 35% of members here).
- **Other indicators.** `same_patient` is strong for systematic (31 of 33 (94%)) and weak for opportunistic (16 of 33 (48%)); `drift` finds template-driven upcoders (17 of 22 (77%), whose selection skews late) and little else; `excess_usd` (dollars, not rate) is 35 of 88 (40%) because it rewards big panels, though it holds 70% of the dollars. Group-level scoring is not possible: the detector view carries no tax ID.
- **Rankers** (logistic over the indicators' percentiles, leave-one-seed-out): `ranker_new` 86 of 88 (98%), `ranker_all` 86 of 88 (98%). They are fitted on the planted labels of the development seeds (a stand-in for past audits), so they need real audit outcomes before they mean anything; not chosen.
- **Not done here:** the issue's hierarchical model of P(effect > margin) is approximated by the empirical-Bayes shrinkage; unsupervised outlier scores and adaptive sampling of the records budget were not run (the second belongs to the arms in #128); a cutoff by expected net dollars is left to the ask policy.
- **The list is not the bottleneck under today's rules.** [screens_v4_pipeline.md](screens_v4_pipeline.md) runs the frozen workflow v2.3 (API-free) on each list: better lists let it find more upcoders (from 3 of 88 to 16) but still few of the up to 81 listed, because the frozen rules and policy, fitted on 99215 evidence in data 2.2, were not built for these habits; it also calls more honest providers, and score 4.0 stays about the same (every row is negative). A v4.0 and v5.0 decision layer that reads the 99214 boundary is #128's work.

## Chosen screen

**`eb_upper_t`** is the default screen of the v4/v5 line (workflow `flagging.py --method eb_upper_t`; the agent's `screen_providers` gets it when agent v5.0 is built in #128): best recall and dollar capture among the indicators that do not need audit labels, and the same shrinkage and test-line lineage as `eb_ratio_t`, so it is a change of threshold (99214 instead of 99215) and not a new kind of score. Fallback if the lab link proves too strong: `eb_upper_c`. Confirm on fresh seeds only, in #128.
