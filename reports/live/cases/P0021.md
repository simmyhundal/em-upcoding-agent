# P0021: P0021 (Family Practice, GA): 99215 share is far above expected, but sampled documentation largely supports the billed level

**Leaning (advisory):** Pattern consistent with a high-acuity panel

## Why

P0021 billed 99215 on 21.5% of 130 claims, against an expected 2.8% given patient complexity (z=2.46, risk-adjusted score 4.78). The excess shows at every complexity level, including patients with 0 chronic conditions (12.2% vs 2.1% for all providers). The low-complexity 99215s include visits for abdominal pain (C0011031, C0011046) and back pain (C0011002), which is the usual concern. However, the records review cuts the other way. Only 1 of 10 documented 99215 claims was unsupported (10%), below the all-provider rate of 24.2%. That claim, C0011002, was documented at moderate MDM and 30 minutes. Some documented 99215s on a low-complexity patient were supported: C0011032 (osteoarthritis plus a URI code) was documented at high MDM and 48 minutes. Others such as C0011068, C0011024 and C0011071 were documented at high MDM with 43 to 52 minutes. The 99213 and 99214 unsupported rates are also below peers (0% and 3.6%). On the evidence so far, the pattern is more consistent with visits that are genuinely complex but not captured by chronic-condition counts than with upcoding. Two caveats limit this: only 10 documented 99215s exist (about 36% of 28), and the records review does not say which of the 13 listed low-complexity 99215s were documented apart from C0011002 and C0011032. This leaning is tentative, and the review should not be closed.
